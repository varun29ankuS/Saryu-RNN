THE CONFOUND. At a fixed 1200-step budget Saryu never clears 0.40 -- but it reaches 0.93 by
2250-3000 steps (memory_on.txt, memory_optimizer.txt). So "narrow convergent region" and "slow
convergence" are not separable from this table. A slower model looks like it has a narrower region
at ANY fixed budget, and that is exactly what the design measures.

THE DEEPER DESIGN ERROR. Sohl-Dickstein's boundary is CONVERGED versus DIVERGED -- parameters
growing without bound. This measured SOLVED versus UNSOLVED, which is a different thing. Nothing
here diverged: every run at every rate up to 3e-2 completed with finite loss. In his sense both
models have a wide stability region and the fractal framing has not been tested at all.

AND IT CORRECTS OUR OWN RECORD. matrix_decision.txt states "DeltaNet needs 3e-3; Saryu diverges
there (loss 4.03) and at 1e-2 (4.18). 1e-3 was already its stability limit." But ln(64) = 4.159 is
CHANCE loss. A loss of 4.03 is not divergence, it is a model sitting at chance. That file used
"diverges" to mean "fails to learn", and this experiment was motivated by repeating it.

WHAT SURVIVES, WEAKLY. Saryu's usable rate tops out about 3x lower: best at 1e-3 and dead by 3e-3,
where DeltaNet peaks at 3e-3 and retains signal at 1e-2. Consistent with a lower stability
ceiling, but not separable from the budget confound on one seed.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY TEST IT. Either run each rate to convergence rather than to a fixed budget --
expensive, since Saryu needs 2250+ steps and the sweep is 7 rates -- or measure divergence
directly by looking for loss growth and parameter-norm blow-up rather than task success. The
second is cheap and is what the fractal result is about. This file should not be cited as
evidence about stability margin until one of those is done.

SO BOTH PUZZLES REMAIN OPEN. The 1.8x excess steps over a faithful DeltaNet, and the bimodality.
The optimizer is ruled out for the first; stability margin is untested rather than ruled out.
