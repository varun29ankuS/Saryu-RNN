ARITY vs CAPACITY  total state D = 64 for BOTH arms, 400 trials, 2 reflections per bind
single  = one factor of D, all pairs summed, one matched filter
product = n slots of D/n (narrower!), one pair each, compared all at once

 pairs   chance    single   product    slot dim
--------------------------------------------------
     2    0.500     0.948     1.000          32
     3    0.333     0.580     1.000          21
     4    0.250     0.410     1.000          16
     8    0.125     0.195     1.000           8

READ
  single high at n=2 then collapsing to 1/n  -> one filter, one bit: ARITY
  product staying high on NARROWER slots     -> factorisation is what matters
  both collapsing together                   -> capacity, and the arity account fails

=============================================================================
READING IT

Both arms hold exactly D = 64 numbers of state. The ONLY difference is whether those 64
numbers are one factor or n factors. The product arm is additionally handicapped: at n = 8
its slots are 8-dimensional, eight times narrower than the single arm's whole state.

  single   0.948 -> 0.580 -> 0.410 -> 0.195   decaying toward chance as n grows
  product  1.000 at every n, on narrower slots

So the binding limit is FACTORISATION, not capacity. A bigger state does not help; a state
split into separately addressable parts does, even when each part is small. That is the
arity claim, tested with capacity held fixed.

All three registered predictions hold. The falsifier -- both arms collapsing together --
did not fire.

ONE WRINKLE, RECORDED BECAUSE IT MATTERS. By construction the single arm degrades GRADUALLY
(0.948, 0.580, 0.410, 0.195) while the TRAINED model collapses to exactly 1/n (0.345 at
n=3, 0.208 at n=4). So training does not even reach the single-filter ceiling its own
mechanism allows. Two limits compound:

  arity        structural, and the subject of this test
  escape time  training never finds the best available single-filter solution at n >= 3
               (rank_dynamics: no escape in 15,000 steps at n = 3 or 4)

Fixing arity alone would lift the ceiling; whether training then reaches it is a separate
question, and the escape-time evidence says it may not without the optimiser work too.

=============================================================================
THE PARTITION TRADE-OFF: ARITY IS BOUNDED BY D / (MINIMUM BLOCK WIDTH)

Same test, D = 64 held fixed, sweeping how finely the state is partitioned:

  pairs   slot dim   single   product
      2       32      0.948    1.000
      4       16      0.410    1.000
      8        8      0.195    1.000
     16        4      0.095    0.848
     32        2      0.052    0.090
     64        2      0.013    0.007

The product arm is perfect down to 8-wide blocks, frays at 4 (0.848) and collapses at 2
(0.090). So a partition cannot be made arbitrarily fine: there is a MINIMUM VIABLE BLOCK
WIDTH, below which a block can no longer hold a value distinguishably, and

    addressable items  ~  D / w_min

At D = 64 with w_min ~ 8 that is about 8 items, not 64.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR HOW THE FIELD FRAMES IT. Zoology and Based report that a bigger
recurrent state gives better recall, and that is correct. But the mechanism here is not that
a bigger state holds a bigger superposition -- the single-factor arm has the WHOLE 64
dimensions and still decays to 0.195 at n = 8. A bigger state helps because it can be cut
into more blocks. Size matters instrumentally, through the partition.

THE DESIGN QUESTION IS THEREFORE A PARTITION, LITERALLY. Choosing how to spend D is choosing
an integer composition D = d_1 + ... + d_k: k blocks buys arity k, each block gets width
d_i, and the two trade against each other with a floor at w_min.

NESTING BUYS WHAT FLATNESS CANNOT. A flat partition of 64 addresses ~8 items. A NESTED
partition -- a refinement chain in the partition lattice, 8 groups each holding 8 -- reaches
8 x 8 = 64 items while every block stays 8 wide, because it spends DEPTH instead of WIDTH.
That is the same trade a page table or a trie makes, and it is why the L = 2 / 4 / 6 depth
runs matter: sequential selection is depth-bounded, so a nested scheme needs the layers to
chain.

=============================================================================
NESTING WORKS BY CONSTRUCTION; DEPTH ALONE DOES NOT DELIVER IT

BY CONSTRUCTION, D = 64 held fixed for every arm:

  pairs   chance   single   product(flat)   nested   flat block   nest block
      8   0.125    0.195      1.000         1.000        8           21
     16   0.062    0.095      0.848         1.000        4           16
     32   0.031    0.052      0.090         1.000        2           10
     64   0.016    0.013      0.007         1.000        2            8

A flat partition spends WIDTH and dies at ~8 items, its blocks collapsing to width 2. A
nested partition spends DEPTH: 64 items with blocks still 8 wide. Same 64 numbers of state.
(An earlier version of this arm scored ~0.2 because the address digits were extracted
little-endian and rebuilt big-endian, reversing the index. Fixed.)

IN THE TRAINED MODEL, DEPTH DOES NOT CHAIN. 4 pairs, muon lr 3e-4, only depth varied:

  depth   steps    max sigma1   final acc   diag mass   escape
      2   15,000     0.0199       0.203      0.2510     NEVER
      4   12,000     0.0258       0.260      0.2490     NEVER
      6    9,500     0.0190       0.219      0.2500     NEVER

Tripling the depth changes nothing. All three sit at the exact barycentre with sigma1 on the
noise floor. By the nesting arithmetic L = 6 has 6 bits of addressing budget against the
4.58 that 4 pairs needs -- it has the budget and does not use it.

WHY, AND WHAT IT IMPLIES. Layers here read the same residual stream and write back to it in
parallel; nothing makes layer k's read CONDITIONAL on layer k-1's selection. Hierarchical
addressing needs exactly that conditionality -- resolve the group, THEN resolve within it --
and stacking layers does not create it. The probe said the same thing from the other side:
at 2 pairs layer 0 contributes nothing (0.032) while layer 1 does all the binding (0.998).
An idle layer is not a second selection.

So nesting requires explicit ROUTING machinery -- the later read must be a function of the
earlier selection -- not merely more depth. That is a specific architectural proposal rather
than a hyperparameter, and it is the honest end of this thread: the arithmetic is right, the
by-construction result is right, and the trained model cannot reach it as currently built.
