THE KNEE WAS A BUDGET ARTEFACT. RETRACTED. AND THE REAL FINDING IS ABOUT SAMPLE EFFICIENCY.

WHAT I CLAIMED. That the matrix memory has a knee at gap 8 and therefore that its range is bounded
by the gate's retention -- "capacity and persistence are in series, not independent". Committed,
then suspended when the convergence curve showed gap 8 still rising when stopped.

WHAT THE CONVERGED RUN SHOWS. Run to 9000 steps, gap 8 crosses 0.8 at step 4250 and converges to
0.992, plateauing from step 8000. There is no knee and no limit -- the memory SOLVES 4 pairs at
gap 8. It needed roughly three times the training it was given. The series claim is WITHDRAWN.

STEPS TO CROSS 0.8, memory=True, 4 pairs, nl=2:

    gap 0    1000
    gap 4    2500
    gap 8    4250

That is roughly linear: about 400 additional steps per token of gap. It explains both_fixes.txt
entirely without any architectural claim -- extrapolating, gap 16 needs ~7,400 steps and it was
given 1,500, which is why every arm there sat near 1/64.

THE COMPARISON THAT MATTERS, AND IT IS THE WORST NUMBER THIS PROJECT HAS PRODUCED

    transformer,      gap 16, 1,500 steps  ->  0.992   (bf-tf-p4-g16-s1)
    Saryu + memory,   gap  8, ~8,000 steps  ->  0.992   (crosses 0.8 at 4,250)

Five times the training for half the distance, to reach the same score. On SAMPLE EFFICIENCY -- the axis this project
exists to win, and the one it has never measured -- the transformer is ahead by roughly an order of
magnitude on this task.

That is a single task, a single seed each, and the transformer was bimodal (0.398 on its other
seed), so it is not a clean measurement. But it is the first time the two have been compared on
tokens-to-solve rather than final accuracy, and the direction is not the one the project's thesis
needs. docs/learning_machine.md section 4 pre-registers the proper version of this measurement and
its falsifier; this is a reason to run it rather than a substitute for it.

METHOD NOTE. Three conclusions in a row today were drawn from runs that had not converged: the
both_fixes factorial, the state_volume void test, and this knee. All three were caught by looking
at the convergence CURVE rather than the final number. Making that routine: no capability limit is
to be claimed from a run that was still improving when it stopped.

*** CAVEAT ADDED BEFORE THIS IS USED: THE GAP-8 POINT WAS NOT CONVERGED. ***
The convergence curves make the step requirement grow steeply with gap:
    gap 0  crosses 0.8 at step 1000
    gap 4  crosses 0.8 at step 2500   (at step 1500 it was still 0.23)
    gap 8  0.18 at step 2000, best 0.328 by 3000, STILL RISING when stopped
So gap 8 plausibly needs 5,000-6,000 steps, and 0.328 is a LOWER BOUND rather than a ceiling. The
"knee" below may be a budget artefact. Reading a capability limit off an under-trained run is the
error shape this project has hit repeatedly, and it is not settled until gap 8 is run to
convergence. The conclusion drawn below -- that capacity and persistence are in series -- is
SUSPENDED pending that run.
memory=True, 4 pairs, nl=2, d=128 H=8 nh=2, 3000 steps, chance 0.250.

    gap    seq    best        solved>0.8
      0     10    1.000       1/1        (a second seed reached 0.984 before being killed)
      4     14    0.930       1/1        reproduces memory_on.txt exactly (0.930, 0.938)
      8     18    0.328       0/1
     16     26    0.039       0/2        (both_fixes.txt, at 1500 steps)

P1 HOLDS. Gap 4 gives 0.930 against memory_on's 0.930/0.938, so the collapse in both_fixes.txt was
the 1500-step budget and not something else about that harness.

P2 HOLDS. This is a knee, not a decline. 0.930 to 0.328 between gap 4 and gap 8 -- a drop of 0.60
across four tokens -- then a slow fade to 0.039.

P3 HOLDS. The knee sits at gap 8, which is where the gap first exceeds what the gate retains.
gate_retention.txt measured retention falling to 8 tokens after 1500 steps and 4 after 6000, with
training driving it DOWN because an unsolvable task makes holding state pure variance.

WHAT THIS MEANS, AND IT CHANGES THE PICTURE. nh_sweep_shortgap.txt separated capacity and
persistence as two axes, and this project has treated them as independent ever since. They are not
independent: they are IN SERIES. The matrix memory gives the state an item axis, so four facts can
coexist -- but those facts still live in a state the gate is overwriting, and the memory's useful
RANGE is bounded by the gate's retention. Fixing capacity alone buys recall out to roughly the
retention horizon and no further.

That is why memory=True scores 0.930 at gap 4 and 0.039 at gap 16, and it predicts that the gate
lower bound should EXTEND the memory's range rather than add to it independently.

WHERE THE COMPOSITION TEST BELONGS. Gap 8. Capacity is still partly handled there (0.328 against
0.250 chance) and persistence has just started to bind, so an interaction between the two fixes can
actually show. both_fixes.py ran at gap 16, where nothing works at all, which is why its 2x2 was
uninformative.

SCOPE. One seed at gaps 4 and 8, two at gap 0. The knee is a 0.60 drop and unlikely to be seed
noise, but it is one seed. nl=2, one task, one width.
