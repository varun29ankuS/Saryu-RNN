VERDICT: keep AdamW.
1. Loss is a tie. Best AdamW 1.6525 against best Muon 1.6548 at context 128 (0.0023), and 1.5649
   against 1.5656 at 512 (0.0007). Both gaps are far inside the seed spread measured at 1M
   (0.02 for AdamW, 0.006 for Muon; see optimizer_local.txt).
2. Muon costs 12.5% more wall clock per step (0.234-0.237 against 0.208-0.210 s/step), which is the
   Newton-Schulz orthogonalisation. At matched compute AdamW wins; at matched steps it is a draw.
3. Muon is more rate-sensitive here: 0.04 loses 0.049 bpc against its own best, while AdamW's worst
   rate loses 0.019. Its best rate sat at the BOTTOM edge of the swept range, so a lower rate
   (0.005, 0.0025) might close the loss gap -- it would still carry the 12.5% tax.
4. Consistent with the literature: Muon's documented advantage appears at large batch sizes beyond
   the critical batch size (Shah et al. 2025, 2505.02222). Our batch is 48 x 128 = 6,144 tokens,
   far below that regime, so a tie is the expected result rather than a surprise.

WHEN TO REVISIT: if batch size grows by an order of magnitude, or at >=100M parameters. Then sweep
rates BELOW 0.01, use the cheap cubic orthogonalisation (Huang 2026, 2606.00371: training quality
is not monotone in polar accuracy), and consider the Adam/Muon hybrid (Zhang et al. 2026,
2602.17080) rather than plain Muon. MuonClip's QK-clip does not apply: Saryu has no attention.

SIDE OBSERVATION: every arm repeats its 512 value at 2048 and 8192, reproducing at 5M with a fresh
training run the saturation measured on the released checkpoints (effective_context.txt).
