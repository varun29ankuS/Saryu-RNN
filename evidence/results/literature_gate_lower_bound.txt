THE GATE COLLAPSE IS A KNOWN FAILURE WITH A PUBLISHED FIX, AND OUR THREE ATTEMPTS ALL MISSED IT
Checked 2026-09-20, immediately after gate_retention.py measured the collapse directly.

WHAT WE MEASURED FIRST (gate_retention.txt, 1 pair, gap 64, d_h = 16):

    at initialisation      ~25 tokens of retention (1/g)
    after 1500 steps         8
    after 6000 steps         4

Training drives retention DOWN, monotonically, away from the 64 tokens the task requires. Not a
capacity limit: one single fact, nothing to interfere with it. The model is descending into a
competing minimum -- discard everything -- which is locally optimal and globally useless.

WHAT THE LITERATURE CALLS THIS. Forget-gate collapse in gated RNNs is a known training failure, and
the standard remedy is a LOWER BOUND on the forget gate that increases with DEPTH.

HGRN (Qin et al., NeurIPS 2023; mechanism restated in HGRN2, COLM 2024, section 2.2):

    Gamma in R^{L x d}   a DATA-INDEPENDENT LEARNABLE matrix; Gamma_i is the lowest value the
                         forget gate of layer i may take, at all time steps.

    beta := cumax(Gamma) = cumsum(softmax(Gamma, dim=0), dim=0),   beta^i = [beta]_i in R^d

    all beta are then shifted by beta^0 so the first layer's bound is zero and the top layer's
    cannot reach one. The forget gate then learns RESIDUALS above the bound:

        f_t^i = beta^i + (1 - beta^i) * g_t^i
        h_t^i = f_t^i * h_{t-1}^i + (1 - f_t^i) * i_t^i

    "HGRN argues that this lower bound should be monotonically increasing from bottom to top,
     encouraging the bottom layers to model short-term local dependencies and the upper layers to
     model long-term dependencies."
    "This additive lower bound approach has been shown to mitigate the issue of saturated gates."

WHY OUR THREE ATTEMPTS FAILED, WHICH IS NOW EXACTLY DIAGNOSABLE. All three are in model.py and all
three are recorded as failures in gate_timescales.txt. Each differs from HGRN on the points that
matter:

  attempt 1  `timescales`         per-HEAD log-spaced bias ladder gh = logspace(0.5, 1e-4, H),
                                  IDENTICAL IN EVERY LAYER. Training moved the bias; every head
                                  ended at 2-4 tokens.
  attempt 2  `freeze_gate_bias`   froze the bias so the ladder could not be destroyed. Training
                                  grew the INPUT weights instead and routed around it.
  attempt 3  `gate_ceiling`       g = torch.minimum(g, g_max), a HARD CLAMP, per head, per-layer
                                  identical.

  FOUR DIFFERENCES, and the last two are the ones that matter most:

    axis          ours varies the bound across HEADS within a layer; HGRN varies it across LAYERS.
                  Our own arm D is independent evidence for the layer axis: at L=4 the long-retention
                  head appeared in LAYER 2, which is also where trained_geometry.txt found the 86-
                  and 132-token heads on the shipped 25M checkpoint. Two layers never produced one
                  at any step count.
    learnable     ours is a fixed constant ladder; HGRN's Gamma is a trained parameter.
    monotone      ours is monotone only at initialisation and training can undo it; cumax makes the
                  bound monotone BY CONSTRUCTION -- a cumulative sum of a softmax is non-decreasing,
                  so no gradient step can break the ordering.
    gradient      ours enforces the bound by CLAMPING, and torch.minimum has ZERO GRADIENT wherever
                  the clamp is active, so the moment the bound binds it stops teaching anything.
                  HGRN reparameterises: f = beta + (1-beta)*g keeps the bound differentiable at all
                  times. That is precisely the "mitigate saturated gates" claim, and it is the
                  difference between a constraint the optimiser learns through and one it dies
                  against.

  Note the clamp problem is a repeat. The flip-flop experiment earlier in this project was recorded
  VOID because its gate clamp never fired and both arms were bit-identical. Clamps have now cost us
  two experiments.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PROJECT

  The gap-64 retention wall was recorded as architectural. It is a known trainability failure with a
  published, cheap, architectural remedy that we tried three variants of and got wrong each time on
  the same two axes.

  This is the THIRD time in this session that a published design was reproduced without a component
  its own paper names as load-bearing:
    1. DeltaNet without the short causal convolution        0.094 -> 0.961 once added
    2. the HRR cell without cleanup                          under test now
    3. a gate ladder without a per-layer differentiable bound  this file

  The common shape is not ignorance of the literature -- all three papers were in refs.bib. It is
  reimplementing the headline mechanism and dropping the part that makes it train.

  IMPORTANT SCOPE LIMIT. This does not reopen the matrix decision. The read is a separate axis, and
  the crossover (matrix 0.984 vs vector 0.023 at 8 pairs) was measured at gap 4, where retention is
  not the binding constraint. What it reopens is the RETENTION axis, which was closed on the
  strength of runs whose gates we had never inspected.

WHAT TO DO, AND THE FALSIFIER
  Implement the HGRN-style bound in SaryuV3Block: a learnable Gamma over layers, cumax for
  monotonicity, additive residual gate, replacing the clamp. Then rerun 1 pair at gap 64.
  FALSIFIER: if retention still collapses below 64 with the differentiable per-layer bound in
  place, the gate is genuinely not the constraint and gate_timescales.txt's conclusion -- "the write
  rule is the constraint, not the gate" -- stands for MQAR as well as for enwik8.
