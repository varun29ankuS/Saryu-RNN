IS IT EXPRESSIVITY OR OPTIMISATION? Expressivity -- and nh=2 is the ceiling.
===========================================================================

THE QUESTION. Nine mechanisms this session were shown to work by construction and none survived
training, which looked like an optimisation problem: the architecture can represent the solution
and gradient descent will not find it. Fable's review named the test that settles it -- hand-build
the MQAR solution in the parameter space. If no setting achieves it, the limit is EXPRESSIVITY. If
a setting exists and training misses it, the limit is OPTIMISATION. These need opposite programmes
of work and every other experiment here is downstream of the answer.

THE CONSTRUCTION. The exact shipped recurrence, h_t = (1-g) H(u_t,beta_t) h_{t-1} + g c_t, with
every quantity SET BY HAND and nothing trained:
  - entity embeddings: random unit vectors
  - binding: the value is reflected by nh MUTUALLY ORTHOGONAL axes derived deterministically from
    the preceding key (an exact involution, trace dh-2nh, so it is its own inverse at read)
  - transport: beta = 0 everywhere except the query position
  - read: beta = 2 on the query key's axes -- the transport IS the unbinding
  - readout: nearest entity embedding
This is the best use of the only key-times-value interaction the parameterisation offers.

RESULT. Accuracy with no training, 400 trials:

  dh = 128           n=2     n=4     n=8    n=16
    nh=1   tr/dh .984   0.525   0.320   0.188   0.075
    nh=2   tr/dh .969   0.560   0.347   0.195   0.075   <- SHIPPED
    nh=8   tr/dh .875   0.897   0.550   0.285   0.138

    nh=8   tr/dh .828   0.945   0.637   0.360   0.142

    nh=46  tr/dh .011   1.000   1.000   0.897   0.505

THE ANSWER. At nh = 2 NO hand-built solution reaches four pairs: the ceiling is 0.347 at dh=128 and
0.325 at dh=93. The TRAINED model reaches 0.21-0.25 on the same task. Training is therefore at
60-72% of the constructible optimum, not a factor of three below it.

  IT IS EXPRESSIVITY. The model is near the limit of what two reflections can represent, and the
  nine "available but unused" mechanisms were never sufficient at nh=2. This REVERSES the
  optimisation reading recorded in flipflop_gate_cap.txt, binding_vs_tracking.txt and
  composition.txt, which each treated a construction that worked as proof that training was the
  gap. The constructions worked because they were free to use as many reflections as they liked.

  The fix is quantified rather than guessed: nh ~ dh/4 gives 0.97 at four pairs, nh ~ dh/2 gives
  1.000. For the shipped checkpoint that is nh = 23 to 46 against the 2 it has.

WHY THE TRACE LAW ALREADY SAID THIS. tr(H)/d = 1 - 2nh/dh is how unlike the identity the operator
is. At nh=2, dh=93 it is 0.957: the binding barely moves a vector, so the read cannot annihilate
the non-matching items. Paper B publishes this law. We derived it, published it, and then spent the
week testing capacity, geometry, gating, routing and optimisers without applying it to nh.

OPEN / NOT CLAIMED -- and the caveat here is load-bearing
  - THIS IS ONE CONSTRUCTION. Failing to build a solution at nh=2 is not a proof that none exists.
    The evidence that it is a real ceiling is circumstantial but consistent: the trained model lands
    at the same place, and the trace law explains why.
  - The construction covers REFLECTION-based binding. The real model has an FFN and a depthwise
    conv, so the write c can be an arbitrary nonlinear function of the last few tokens -- it could
    in principle compute a multiplicative binding that is not a reflection. That family is NOT
    tested here and is the obvious way the expressivity conclusion could be wrong.
  - Idealised embeddings, no conv, no FFN, single layer, single head.
  - The cost of the fix is not free: nh ~ dh/2 reflections cost O(dh^2/2) per head, which is
    matrix-state cost. trained_geometry.txt records that a permutation buys direction for O(d);
    whether something similarly cheap buys separation is unknown.
