CAN THE MEMORY COMBINE TWO FACTS INTO A THIRD? Direction is the whole story.
===========================================================================

THE GAP THIS CLOSES. Every test in this repository stores facts and asks for one back. Nothing has
ever asked the memory to COMBINE two stored facts into one it was never shown. That is a different
capability from recall and we had no measurement of it.

The clean form is transitive inference: store A->B and B->C, query A, expect C. It needs something
MQAR never does -- CHAINING two retrievals, which means using depth as sequential lookup. The
linear probe showed binding forms at layer 1's mix while layer 2 sits idle, so there was a specific
reason to expect chaining to fail.

SETUP. HRR store, d = 1024, m = 256 unitary symbols, n stored edges x -> y, follow a k-step chain
never stored as a single fact. Cleanup (snap to the nearest known symbol) at every hop.

RESULT 1 -- COMMUTATIVE BINDING CANNOT CHAIN, AND THE REASON IS EXACT.

     n edges   k |    comm      dir
           8   1 |   0.980    0.980
           8   2 |   0.503    0.970
           8   3 |   0.140    0.977
           8   4 |   0.033    0.970
           8   6 |   0.000    0.973

          32   1 |   0.897    0.943
          32   2 |   0.417    0.887
          32   3 |   0.080    0.820
          32   6 |   0.003    0.710

          64   1 |   0.690    0.780
          64   2 |   0.253    0.600
          64   3 |   0.047    0.517
          64   6 |   0.003    0.243

  comm = S = sum x (*) y.  Circular convolution is COMMUTATIVE, so x0 (*) x1 = x1 (*) x0 and a
  stored edge HAS NO DIRECTION. Unbinding at x1 returns its predecessor x0 and its successor x2
  with equal weight: the chain cannot tell forward from backward, which is why k=2 lands on 0.503,
  a coin flip, even though k=1 is perfect. Not noise accumulation -- a symmetry.

  dir = S = sum x (*) rho(y), a fixed coordinate permutation applied to the target. That breaks the
  symmetry, and chain length becomes nearly free: flat at ~0.97 out to six hops at n=8. Capacity
  still binds at larger n (0.24 at n=64, k=6), but commutative binding is dead by hop three at
  every size.

  Using a permutation for roles is standard VSA practice (Plate, Kanerva, Gayler), so "direction
  matters" is not a new claim in that literature. What is worth recording is the size of it: it is
  the difference between no compositional capability at all and six free hops.

RESULT 2 -- RETRACTED AND REVERSED. THE MEMORY CAN VERIFY, VIA THE MARGIN.
  I first wrote: "retrieval confidence barely separates correct from wrong derivations (0.012 at
  n=32 k=3), so the memory produces a novel combination and has no signal for whether it is true."
  That was a DIFFERENCE OF MEANS on max codebook similarity -- the wrong statistic on the wrong
  signal. Scored properly as AUC (the probability the signal ranks a correct derivation above a
  wrong one, 0.50 = no information):

      n    k     acc   max sim   margin   min margin   round trip
     16    2   0.953     0.703    0.960        0.974        0.654
     16    3   0.938     0.744    0.976        0.959        0.686
     16    4   0.910     0.881    0.964        0.983        0.800
     32    2   0.890     0.637    0.888        0.873        0.639
     32    3   0.827     0.748    0.919        0.893        0.655
     32    4   0.802     0.821    0.903        0.926        0.775
     64    2   0.615     0.743    0.788        0.793        0.630
     64    3   0.505     0.815    0.808        0.773        0.655
     64    4   0.382     0.836    0.793        0.781        0.678

  MARGIN (top-1 minus top-2 similarity) reaches AUC 0.96-0.98 where MAX SIMILARITY manages
  0.70-0.88. The reason is structural: in a superposition the absolute overlap is dominated by the
  store's total energy, so it says how much is in memory rather than whether THIS answer is right.
  The margin is scale-free and asks the question that matters -- was the runner-up close?

  MIN MARGIN ACROSS HOPS is as good or better on long chains (0.983 at n=16, k=4), because it
  catches a wrong turn at any point rather than only at the end.

  ROUND TRIP (walk back from the endpoint and check it returns to the start) is WEAKER than the
  margin, 0.63-0.80, which I did not expect -- the reverse walk has its own error rate, so it adds
  noise as well as evidence.

  The corrected claim: a superposed memory CAN tell its true combinations from its false ones, at
  AUC ~0.97 on moderate stores, provided the signal is the margin rather than the confidence. This
  degrades with load (0.79 at n=64) but stays informative even where accuracy has fallen to 0.38.

RESULT 3 -- IS A HOUSEHOLDER ROLE OPERATOR ENOUGH TO GIVE DIRECTION? Yes, above a threshold.
  Replacing the permutation with a product of `nref` MUTUALLY ORTHOGONAL Householder reflections
  applied to the target (an exact involution, so it is its own inverse). d = 1024, n = 16 edges:

      role operator      tr(R)/d     k=1     k=2     k=3     k=4     k=6
      1 reflection         0.998   0.960   0.468   0.204   0.072   0.004
      2 reflections        0.996   0.940   0.484   0.172   0.056   0.004
      8 reflections        0.984   0.928   0.496   0.220   0.068   0.008
      32 reflections       0.938   0.960   0.716   0.480   0.300   0.164
      128 reflections      0.750   0.964   0.932   0.916   0.888   0.872
      512 (= d/2)          0.000   0.988   0.960   0.936   0.904   0.900
      permutation            --    0.980   0.952   0.932   0.892   0.880
      commutative            --    0.972   0.492   0.100   0.032   0.000

  The controlling quantity is tr(R)/d = 1 - 2*nref/d, how unlike the identity the role operator is.
  One or two reflections in 1024 dimensions leave it at 0.998 and chaining is indistinguishable
  from commutative. Somewhere past tr/d ~ 0.94 direction appears, and by 0.75 it matches a
  permutation.

  WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE SHIPPED MODEL. Reflections act inside a head, dh = d//H = 16, with
  nh = 2, so tr(R)/dh = 1 - 4/16 = 0.750 -- exactly the row that reaches permutation-level
  chaining. Per head, Saryu's reflections are ALREADY strong enough to break the direction
  symmetry. The ratio nh/dh is what matters, not nh, and this project has repeatedly swept nh while
  holding dh fixed without noticing that the relevant quantity is their ratio.

  IMPORTANT CAVEAT. This test orthogonalises the axes via QR. v_proj normalises each axis and never
  orthogonalises them against each other, and non-orthogonal axes do not give an involution
  (measured earlier: error 1.06 at k=8), so the decode would not be exact. Orthogonality is a
  second requirement and the model does not meet it.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR SARYU SPECIFICALLY
  HOUSEHOLDER REFLECTIONS DO NOT COMMUTE. Non-commutativity is the property this project has spent
  a week treating as the reason the architecture is good at state tracking and useless for memory.
  Result 1 says a commutative binding is precisely what CANNOT support directed relational memory,
  and direction is what chaining needs. So the architecture's signature property is the one a
  convolution store lacks.

  This is a statement about potential, not about the shipped model. Saryu applies its reflections as
  TRANSPORT to the whole state, not as a per-fact binding role, so the non-commutativity is not
  currently encoding direction for anything. It is the EIGHTH mechanism this session found to be
  present, reachable, and unused by training -- after the idle layer, the level-2 term, the gate,
  the per-head routing, orthogonal axes, selective writing, and the delta write.

BUG FOUND AND FIXED IN MY OWN HARNESS
  The first version of this test had a `raw` (no-cleanup) arm whose final readout correlated the
  estimate against a ZERO vector, so it returned 0.000 for every configuration. That column was
  meaningless and is not reported. Cleanup-versus-raw is therefore still unmeasured here.

OPEN / NOT CLAIMED
  - Constructions. Nothing trained. Symbols are handed over, not learned.
  - A coordinate permutation is not a Householder reflection. That non-commutativity in general
    preserves direction does not establish that OUR transport does, in the place the model applies
    it. That is the next test and it has not been run.
  - The n=64 degradation is unexplained and no capacity law is fitted.
  - Chain lengths beyond 6 and stores beyond 64 edges are unmeasured.
  - Result 2 uses one confidence statistic (max codebook similarity). A better-calibrated signal
    may exist; the claim is that the obvious one does not work.
