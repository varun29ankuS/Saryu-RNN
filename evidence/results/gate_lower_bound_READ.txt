THE GATE LOWER BOUND FIXES THE RETENTION COLLAPSE. THE FALSIFIER DID NOT FIRE.
1 pair, gap 64, d_h = 16, depth 4, 1500 steps. Chance 0.016.

                            solve rate    max retention 1/g, deepest layers
  D  depth 4, no bound         1/3        L2:669 L3:7 | L2:2 L3:1 | L2:2 L3:1
  E  depth 4, + bound          3/3        L3:1826 | L3:3477 | L3:1306
  E  second launch             3/4        1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 0.008

  Combined: 6 of 7 seeds solve WITH the bound, 1 of 3 WITHOUT.

PROVENANCE, stated because it was not a clean single run. The first launch appeared to die -- three
runs logged, a zero-byte output file, and a task notification reporting exit 0. A replacement was
started for arm E alone. The original was in fact still alive and finished afterwards, so there are
TWO independent measurements of arm E, from separately seeded launches, and they agree. That is
better evidence than one clean run, but it was luck rather than design.

WHAT WAS REGISTERED, in results/literature_gate_lower_bound.txt, before this was built:
  "FALSIFIER: if retention still collapses below 64 with the differentiable per-layer bound in
   place, the gate is genuinely not the constraint and gate_timescales.txt's conclusion -- 'the
   write rule is the constraint, not the gate' -- stands for MQAR as well as for enwik8."

It did not collapse. Layer 3 holds 1,306 to 3,477 tokens against a 64-token gap, where the
unbounded model's deepest layers sat at 1 to 7.

WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES
  The retention half of the gap wall was a TRAINABILITY failure, not an architectural one. The gate
  could always express long memory -- gate_retention.txt measured it being driven DOWN, 25 -> 8 -> 4
  tokens, away from what the task needs. Gradient descent was actively destroying retention because
  once the task is unsolvable, holding state only adds variance to the loss. The bound removes that
  escape, and the model then finds the solution 6 times in 7 instead of 1 time in 3.

WHY THE THREE EARLIER ATTEMPTS FAILED, now confirmed rather than inferred. timescales,
freeze_gate_bias and gate_ceiling all enforced the ladder by CLAMPING or freezing. torch.minimum has
zero gradient wherever it binds, so the constraint stops teaching at the moment it applies, and
training routed around it every time. The working version is multiplicative -- g <- (1-beta)*g -- so
gradient reaches g at every value of the bound, and monotonicity is structural via cumsum-of-softmax
rather than an initialisation training is free to undo.

WHAT IT RETIRES
  gate_timescales.txt concluded "the write rule is the constraint, not the gate". For MQAR
  retention that is now wrong: it was the gate. The enwik8 effective-context finding is a separate
  measurement and is untouched by this.

WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH
  - One task, one configuration, 1500 steps, d_h = 16. Nothing here is about language.
  - The CAPACITY half is untouched. This is 1 pair. nh_sweep_shortgap separated capacity and
    persistence as two axes; the matrix memory addressed capacity, this addresses persistence, and
    no run has yet tested them together at a gap where both bind.
  - The 1/3 baseline for arm D rests on 3 seeds and is bimodal. It is a solve RATE, not a mean.
