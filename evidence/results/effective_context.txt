HOW MUCH CONTEXT DO THE TRAINED CHECKPOINTS ACTUALLY USE?
evidence/effective_context.py, run 2026-09-16 on CPU. Scored text is identical at every context
length: a window of length ctx ENDS at a fixed position and only the last 128 characters are
scored. "KL to ref" is the mean KL between the next-character distributions given ctx characters
and given the longest context; "state cos" is the cosine similarity of the recurrent state entering
the scored region, overall and per layer.

WHAT IT SAYS
1. Both checkpoints stop using context at about 512 characters. Past it the state entering the
   scored region is identical to six decimals in every layer, the predictions are unchanged
   (KL = 0.000000), and the bits per character do not move.
2. The apparent gain from context 128 to 8192 in the published table was two artefacts stacked:
   window starts were drawn separately per length, so each column scored different text; and at
   ctx = 128 the scored characters have NO context in front of them at all, which is why 128 is
   the only row that differs (KL 4.55 at 5M, 8.39 at 25M).
3. The prediction registered in the script's docstring ("if KL and state disagreement collapse
   beyond some length, the results table's claim has to be narrowed to that length") is therefore
   confirmed, and the claim is being narrowed everywhere it appears.
4. The deeper cause is in the architecture, not the evaluation: the write gate is convex, so the
   state is a decaying mixture. The measured mean gate is 0.43 in layer 1 falling to 0.09 in
   layer 4, and (1 - 0.09)^512 is about 1e-21, so nothing from beyond a few hundred characters
   survives. Reaching further needs a gate that can stay near zero for a long span, or the
   separate fact memory in experimental/memory/.
