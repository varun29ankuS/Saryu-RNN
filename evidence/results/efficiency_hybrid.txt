EFFICIENCY, FOUR ARMS, TWO SEEDS. THREE OF THE FOUR REGISTERED PREDICTIONS FAILED.
================================================================================
The one that held is the one that mattered, and the three that failed are worth more
than it.

  P1  Saryu beats a parameter-matched transformer on tokens-to-loss.  HOLDS.
      saryu-plain reaches bpc 3.0 in 204,800 tokens; the transformer needs 1,638,400 on
      its BETTER seed and 2,252,800 on its worse one. Best bpc after 3.07M tokens:
      2.176/2.189 against 2.571/2.751. An 8x token gap against the transformer's best
      showing, and the transformer never reaches bpc 2.5 at all.

  P2  Saryu beats the GRU, so the gain is THIS recurrence and not merely A recurrence.
      FALSIFIED. A GRU with pre-norm and residuals -- the same structural treatment the
      other arms get -- reaches best bpc 2.190/2.193 against saryu-plain's 2.189/2.176.
      That is a tie. Saryu is genuinely ahead early (204,800 tokens to bpc 3.0 against
      409,600; 819,200 to bpc 2.5 against 1,024,000) and ahead on wall-clock, since the
      GRU is the slowest arm per step. But on where the loss ends up, a 1990s
      architecture matches it.

  P3  The matrix memory and gate lower bound help.  FALSIFIED, against us.
      409,600 tokens to bpc 3.0 with them on against 204,800 with them off; best bpc
      2.201 against 2.176. Both were built for MQAR recall capacity, both work there,
      and both cost 2x here.

  P4  The 3:1 hybrid beats the pure recurrence.  NO EFFECT, and the design was at fault.
      2.176/2.293 against 2.189/2.176 -- a tie whose seed spread exceeds the gap. Not a
      failure of the hybrid: at ctx 128 there is nothing to retrieve, so this harness
      cannot see what a 3:1 stack is for. That should have been noticed before the
      prediction was registered, not after it was run.

WHAT THE RESULT ACTUALLY IS, restated without the flattering framing. At 5M parameters,
ctx 128, 3.07M tokens of enwik8, RECURRENT MODELS REACH SUBSTANTIALLY LOWER LOSS THAN A
PARAMETER-MATCHED TRANSFORMER -- both of them, by a wide margin. Saryu's advantage over a
GRU is that it gets there about twice as fast early and costs less per step; it is not
that it ends up better. The headline is recurrence versus attention in a small-data
regime, which is what the literature predicts, and is therefore the least surprising
version of this result rather than the most.

A PROCESS NOTE, because it happened twice inside one hour. The GRU was reported here as
"best 3.051, never reaches 3.0" and then as "the fix barely mattered, 3.027". Both were
reads of a RUNNING job. It finished at 2.190. CLAIMS.md already carries the rule that a
curve still rising when stopped is not a result; it was written after three experiments
were voided for it, and it was then broken twice in consecutive messages on this very
experiment.

STILL NOT ESTABLISHED: two seeds; one learning rate for four architectures when
stability_margin measured them peaking at different rates; a small-data regime; and
nothing converged -- every arm was still improving when stopped.
