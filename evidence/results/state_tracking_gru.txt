THE GRU SOLVES IT TOO. OUR STRONGEST CAPABILITY CLAIM IS ABOUT RECURRENCE, NOT ABOUT US.
================================================================================
conjunctive.txt records the cleanest capability result in this project -- S_3 word
problem, Saryu 1.000/1.000 against a matched transformer's 0.562/0.859, with the
transport causally necessary and the memory causally irrelevant. It has been quoted as
the architecture's strongest evidence. A GRU was never run on it.

    saryu        1.000, 1.000
    gru          1.000, 1.000
    transformer  0.727, 0.703      (chance 0.333)

All three registered predictions hold, including the one that costs us. This is what the
theory says should happen and the expectation was written down before running: fixed-depth
log-precision attention is in TC^0, the word problem for a non-solvable group is
NC^1-complete, and regular languages are not all inside TC^0 -- so the TRANSFORMER is the
arm the complexity argument is about. It says nothing against a GRU, which is a nonlinear
recurrence, i.e. a finite automaton with real-valued state, and finite automata are
exactly what recognises regular languages.

WHAT HAS TO CHANGE. "Saryu solves state tracking where a transformer cannot" is true and
stays. "Saryu has a state-tracking capability that distinguishes it" does not: a 1990s GRU
matches it at 1.000/1.000. The Householder and delta-rule literature is about recovering
this ability in LINEAR RNNs -- which train in parallel -- and not about exceeding
classical nonlinear ones, which already have it and cannot be parallelised.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS NOW THE WHOLE CLAIM. Saryu matches a GRU on capability here and
ties it on enwik8 loss (efficiency_hybrid.txt: 2.176/2.189 against 2.190/2.193), while
being parallel-trainable: 3.7x faster per step than a matched GRU at ctx 2048, and the gap
widens monotonically with length (parallel_advantage.txt). That is a coherent position --
GRU-quality, parallel-trainable -- and it is smaller and more defensible than what this
repository was previously implying.
