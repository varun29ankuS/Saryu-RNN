7. WHY THE REPORTED |ker| COLUMN IS NOISE
   word_problem.py estimates the kernel by clustering  M[g] = mean over random h of T(h,g).
   T is LINEAR in h and h is zero-mean, so M[g] is a Monte-Carlo zero for every g:
   the clustering then runs on sampling noise, which is why it returns orders that
   are not normal subgroup orders at all.
   ||mean_h T(h,g)|| averaged over g : 0.1079
   mean_h ||T(h,g)||   averaged over g : 1.9012
   ratio 0.057  -- the signal the clustering sees is
   5.7% of the transport it is meant to measure;
   the threshold used is 0.05 on distances scaled by sqrt(d).
   on an UNTRAINED model, where all 24 transports are independent random draws
   and the true kernel is trivial, the estimator reports |ker| = 8.0 (3 clusters)
