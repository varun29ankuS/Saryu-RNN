TRACKING AND BINDING ARE ANTAGONISTIC -- and "one vector = one bit" was wrong
=============================================================================

WHERE THIS CAME FROM. Asked to look at nature and biology. The binding problem is a named problem
in neuroscience -- von der Malsburg 1981 called it the SUPERPOSITION CATASTROPHE: a distributed
code that sums "red circle" and "blue square" cannot say which colour went with which shape. The
useful part is what biology does NOT do about it. It does not grow the vector. Dentate gyrus
expands entorhinal input ~5x at ~1-2% sparsity; cerebellar granule cells expand mossy input by
four orders of magnitude (Marr 1969, Albus 1971); fly olfaction maps 50 projection neurons to
~2000 Kenyon cells at ~5% activity (Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha 2017). EXPANSION and
SPARSIFICATION, to make codes pseudo-orthogonal so superposition stops colliding.

TEST 1 -- IS "ONE VECTOR = ONE MATCHED FILTER = ONE BIT" TRUE? No.
  Holographic Reduced Representations (Plate 1995; Kanerva) bind many pairs into ONE vector:
  h = sum_i k_i (*) v_i, unbound by k_q* (*) h. Measured at d = 64, 600 trials:

      binding operator                    n=2     n=3     n=4     n=8
      HRR, circular convolution         1.000   0.998   0.983   0.892
      k Householder reflections, k=2    0.942   0.563   0.420   0.193
      k Householder reflections, k=8    1.000   0.923   0.800   0.440
      k Householder reflections, k=32   1.000   1.000   0.992   0.860

  A single vector of d=64 binds EIGHT pairs at 0.89. So Addendum III step 5 -- "a single matched
  filter extracts one component; this follows from having one vector and one query-dependent
  operator" -- is FALSE. HRR has one vector and one query-dependent operator and extracts one of
  eight. RETRACTED.

  The correct statement: one query-dependent operator selects one item only if the read RANDOMISES
  the non-matching items away. Two Householder reflections randomise almost nothing -- the trace
  law we already publish gives 1 - 2/d per reflection -- so the "matched filter" leaves ~94% of
  every non-matching item sitting in place. "Arity" was the wrong NAME for the measured cliff, not
  a wrong measurement: the product arm and the high-k single arm are two routes to the same thing,
  orthogonality of non-matching items at read time.

ARITHMETIC CORRECTION that kills the obvious fix. model.py:144 sets dh = d // H = 16, and mix()
  returns u as [B,L,H,nh,dh] -- reflections act INSIDE a 16-dim head, not across d=128. So the
  per-reflection trace is 1 - 2/16 = 0.875 and the already-run nh=8 sweep was at 0.875^8 = 0.344
  crosstalk, past the point where the ideal table says n=4 should work. It measured 0.208.
  Therefore nh=32 is NOT the fix and was not run. The ideal-to-trained gap is the whole story.

TEST 2 -- WHAT IS THE SELF-INVERSION CONDITION? k MUTUALLY ORTHOGONAL axes.
  ||(prod_k H)^2 - I||_F / ||I||_F and trace/dh, dh = 16, 200 draws:

       k   random axes   orthogonal axes   trace/dh rand   trace/dh orth
       1        0.0000            0.0000          0.8750          0.8750
       2        0.2590            0.0000          0.7643          0.7500
       4        0.6549            0.0000          0.5871          0.5000
       8        1.0648            0.0000          0.3459         -0.0000

  k mutually orthogonal Householder axes give a reflection through a (dh-k)-subspace: an EXACT
  involution at every k, with trace dh - 2k. At k = dh/2 the trace is exactly 0 -- complete
  decorrelation AND perfect self-inversion at once. Random axes give one or the other, never both.
  This is Kanerva/Gayler sign-flip binding. v_proj normalises each axis and never orthogonalises
  them against each other, so the shipped model has neither property.

  fixed codebook. dh=16, gap 4, 500 trials. Swept the gate across the range the model actually
  learns (it learns g ~ 0.05):

      0.02         0.214       0.122        0.182        0.126

  And with every repair applied at once -- pre-bound write, exact-inverse read, nh raised to dh/2,

      nh=2  random      invert    n=2 0.496  n=3 0.306  n=4 0.174  n=8 0.124
      nh=8  random      invert    n=2 0.480  n=3 0.330  n=4 0.274  n=8 0.126
      nh=8  ORTHOGONAL  invert    n=2 0.480  n=3 0.358  n=4 0.258  n=8 0.136

  TWO BUGS IN THIS ARM, both mine, both found after committing it:
    1. A first pass used gate 0.5, where the first pair decays by 0.5^13 before the query. That
       null measured decay, not binding, and is superseded by the sweep above.
    2. WORSE: each token's BINDING axes and its TRANSPORT axes were the same array. So the query
       token transported the state by H_kq and the read then applied H_kq^-1 -- the two cancel,
       and the read never touched the stored binding at all. The "every repair applied at once"
       null therefore measured nothing. Rerun with the two roles drawn independently; the numbers
       above stand only for the plain arm.

THE BLOCKER -- FIRST STATEMENT WAS WRONG, CORRECTED HERE
  I first wrote: "item i is multiplied by prod_{s>i} H_s, a different operator per item fixed by
  position, so one read-time operation can invert at most one of them -- the transport destroys the
  READ." Test 4's own rows refute that. Transport ON with a bound write and an inverse read gives
  0.230 at n=4; transport OFF with an unbound write gives 0.220. Equal. If the transport were what
  destroyed the read, removing it alone would help. It does not. Only transport-off AND bound write
  reaches 0.402.

  CORRECTED: TWO things are missing, and no arm in this project has ever had both.

    (a) There is no key-value PRODUCT anywhere. The write is c = c_proj(z) plus g, linear in the
        conv window, so nothing multiplicative binds a value to its key. Without that there is
        nothing for a read-time operator to invert, transport or no transport.
    (b) The per-head transport SCRAMBLES whatever binding does get in. At dh=16 with nh=2 random
        axes, prod_{s>i} H_s is ~11 near-identity reflections in a random subspace: enough to break
        the match between the binding operator and its inverse, not enough to decorrelate anything.

  So the accurate claim is that the transport destroys BINDING, not that it destroys the read, and
  it only matters once a binding exists to destroy. That is weaker and more specific than the
  "explains every null in one line" sentence I committed, which is withdrawn: the nulls are
  explained by (a) alone for the arms that had no product, and by (a)+(b) together for carve +
  bind_write, which supplied a write reflection but kept the transport on.

  bind_write in model.py is (a), and it has only ever been tested with the transport ON. The
  transport-free configuration that Test 4 says matters has never been trained.

TEST 3/4 CORRECTED -- transport axes and binding axes drawn INDEPENDENTLY
  (dh=16, nh=2 random, gate 0.05, 500 trials). This supersedes the two tables below, which were
  run with the shared-axes bug.

      transport   bound write     n=2     n=3     n=4     n=8    effect at n=4
           on           off     0.488   0.296   0.254   0.100    (the shipped model)
           on            on     0.536   0.374   0.308   0.146    +0.054
          off           off     0.508   0.304   0.260   0.102    +0.006
          off            on     0.726   0.528   0.442   0.194    +0.188

  The effect is an INTERACTION. A key-value product with the transport still on buys 0.054. Turning
  the transport off with no product buys 0.006 -- nothing. Doing both buys 0.188, more than three
  times the sum of the parts. That is the corrected form of the blocker, and it is why no single
  intervention this session ever moved the number: every one of them supplied at most one half.

  SELECTIVE WRITING is a third factor of the same size. The recurrence writes at EVERY token, so
  keys, separators and fillers all inject noise that scales as sqrt(#writes)/dh. Writing only at
  value positions:

      dh   fillers     n=4     n=8    n=16
      16      yes     0.624   0.354   0.162
      16       no     0.836   0.526   0.252
      64      yes     0.854   0.604   0.330
      64       no     0.996   0.826   0.492

  At dh=64 with selective writes, n=8 reaches 0.826 against HRR's 0.892 -- so most of the gap I
  called "not yet explained" was this artefact, and only a small spectrum term remains. Biology
  gates encoding rather than writing continuously (Hasselmo's acetylcholine switch between encode
  and retrieve in hippocampus). The model can already express this: the write is gw = g * softplus
  (w_raw), so a near-zero write at non-value positions is representable. It is the sixth reachable
  component this session that training does not use.

WHAT BIOLOGY SAYS ABOUT EXACTLY THIS CONFLICT
  It does not solve it in one circuit. Complementary Learning Systems (McClelland, McNaughton &
  O'Reilly 1995): hippocampus does fast, sparse, pattern-separated binding; neocortex does slow
  distributed statistics; they are separate BECAUSE the representations are incompatible -- pattern
  separation and generalisation pull opposite ways. Kanerva's Sparse Distributed Memory (1988) was
  itself a cerebellum model, which is the same line as the VSA algebra above.

  That maps onto the open routes as: heterogeneous heads, some transporting (tracking) and some
  not (binding), with the binding heads WIDER and their axes orthogonalised -- rather than one
  homogeneous head type asked to do both.

TEST 5 -- EXPANSION: widen the binding head. No transport, bound write, nh = dh/2 orthogonal
  (the full involution), 400 trials.

      dh   nh=dh/2      n=2     n=4     n=8    n=16    n=32
      16         8    0.868   0.613   0.375   0.165   0.062
      32        16    0.910   0.733   0.512   0.237   0.107
      64        32    0.943   0.860   0.625   0.340   0.152
     128        64    0.968   0.930   0.755   0.485   0.242
     256       128    0.993   0.975   0.845   0.652   0.320

      current model: dh = 16, nh = 2, WITH transport  ->  0.230 at n = 4

  Capacity grows monotonically with width, as the expansion argument predicts. The shipped
  configuration sits in the worst cell of this table: the narrowest head, the fewest reflections,
  and the transport still on. Changing only the transport and the axis constraint, at the SAME
  dh = 16, already gives 0.613 at n = 4 against the current 0.230.

  It does NOT reach HRR at matched width: dh = 64 gives 0.625 at n = 8 where HRR at d = 64 gives
  0.892. Two causes, one of them an unfair comparison:

    - FILLER NOISE, and it is my arm's fault, not the architecture's. This arm writes a random
      vector at every key, separator and filler position, because the recurrence writes at every
      token. At n = 32 that is 64 key writes plus 4 fillers against 32 value writes, and the noise
      grows as sqrt(#writes)/dh -- which is why n=32 stays at 0.32 even at dh=256. The HRR arm
      writes only values. Quantified in the fillers on/off sweep.
    - SPECTRUM. An involution through a dh/2-subspace has only eigenvalues +-1, so different keys'
      operators stay correlated; circular convolution spreads phase and decorrelates further. This
      part is real and not yet explained.

  So orthogonal-Householder binding is a large improvement on what we ship, and part of the
  remaining gap to convolution is an artefact of comparing a write-every-token recurrence against
  a write-values-only store.
