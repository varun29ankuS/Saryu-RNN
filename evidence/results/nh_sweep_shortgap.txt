WHAT THIS MEANS -- AND WHAT THE PAPERS WERE MISSING

THE CONFOUND IS GONE. The gap-64 sweep (nh_sweep.txt) put every arm at chance, so it
could not tell "n_h does not help" from "nothing learns here". Two controls fixed that:

    1 pair, gap 0    recall 1.000 at n_h=2   -> the harness learns recall perfectly well
    1 pair, gap 64   recall 0.012 / 0.023    -> a 64-token gap is a RETENTION wall
    4 pairs, gap 4   learnable, and the range below is real

THE RESULT REVERSES THE PREDICTION. At a gap the model can handle:

    n_h        2        4        8       16
    recall     0.113    0.047    0.020    0.004        (chance 0.016)
    trace      0.818    0.611    0.364    0.122
    mean beta  1.558    1.880    1.960    1.960

P1 holds: the trace tracks (1-2/d_h)^n_h. P2 does not merely fail, it fails BACKWARDS --
more reflections monotonically DESTROY recall, ending below chance. The registered
prediction was a gain of at least +0.20; the measured gain is -0.109.

WHY, AND THIS IS THE THING WE WERE MISSING. The by-construction result binds a key to a
value, sums the pairs into a state, and reads one back by applying that key's reflections
AGAIN -- reflections are involutions, so the second application unbinds. Two things that
scheme assumes are false in this architecture:

  no unbind step   The recurrence only ever transports forward. Nothing applies a key's
                   inverse at read time, so a bound pair is never undone on purpose.
  the state is re-transported   Every later token applies ITS transport to the WHOLE
                   state. The by-construction scheme assumes a bound pair sits untouched
                   until it is read; here it is hit by every token in between, and those
                   transports do not cancel.

Under those conditions a larger n_h is not extra capacity, it is a faster scrambler --
which is exactly the monotone decay above.

The beta column shows the model reaching the same conclusion by itself. At n_h=2, where
it can still choose, it backs off to beta=1.558, i.e. AWAY from pure reflection, raising
the trace to 0.818 and keeping the state more nearly intact. At n_h=8 and 16 it stays at
beta~1.96 and there is no setting of beta that saves it: with sixteen reflections per
token the state cannot be preserved at all.

WHAT THE PAPERS MUST NOT SAY. "The same transport is a tracker at small k and an
associative store at k ~ d/2" is TRUE of the mechanism in isolation and FALSE of this
recurrence as built. The trace law describes what a product of reflections could store;
it does not license the claim that raising n_h gives this architecture a memory. That
correction is now in paper A Section 5.5 and paper B Section 8.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY BE NEEDED: a read path that applies the inverse transport of the
query, and a way to protect a stored pair from the transports of intervening tokens --
a different architecture, not a different n_h.
