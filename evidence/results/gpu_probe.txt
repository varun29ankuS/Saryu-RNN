GPU PROBE ON A T4. THE WEEK IS FEASIBLE, AND THE PESSIMISTIC PREDICTION WAS WRONG.
================================================================================
Run on Kaggle, Tesla T4, torch 2.10.0+cu128, 8 layers, chunk 32. First time this
architecture has ever executed on a GPU.

P1 FALSIFIED, in our favour. The registered prediction was that a T4 would punish an
unfused eager kernel HARDER than a CPU, because launch overhead is a fixed cost per
operation and ours are small. The opposite happened: the transformer/Saryu throughput
ratio is 1.21-1.63x at batch 32, against 2.0-2.9x measured on CPU. The GPU is KINDER
to this kernel than the laptop was.

P2 HOLDS, and it is the actionable one. Batch size is what closes the gap, because it
amortises launch overhead across more work per call:
    d=328  B=8  ->  3.19x        d=328  B=32  ->  1.63x
    d=768  B=8  ->  1.37x        d=768  B=32  ->  1.21x
Train at the largest batch that fits. That is worth about 2x and costs nothing.

P3 HOLDS. 689M tokens per 9-hour session at d=328/B=32/T=512; 430M at d=512.
The falsifier -- under ~100M tokens per session, meaning no model this week -- did not
fire, by a factor of four at the smallest useful size.

THE REAL CONSTRAINT IS MEMORY, NOT SPEED, and it was not the one being watched. Peak
memory is 2-3x the transformer's at matched settings: 13.56 GB against 4.71 GB at
d=328/B=32/T=1024, and Saryu OOMs at d=512/B=32/T=1024 and d=768/B=32/T=1024 where the
transformer fits in 7-11 GB. The chunk kernel materialises large intermediates -- the
[B,H,N,C,dk] reshapes and the per-chunk Gram matrices -- and nothing has ever been done
about it because no CPU run was big enough to care. That is now the binding limit on
model size, and it is a kernel problem rather than an architectural one.

THE CONFIGURATION THIS SELECTS, on tokens-per-parameter rather than on peak throughput:
    d=512, 8 layers, ~23.7M params, B=32, T=512   ->  430M tokens/session, 9.76 GB peak
Two sessions is ~860M tokens, about 36 tokens per parameter, which is the right side of
Chinchilla for a small model. d=328 is faster per token but the model is too small to
say much; d=768 fits only at batch 8 and drops to 205M tokens/session.
