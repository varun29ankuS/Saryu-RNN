WHERE OUR BINDING RESULT SITS IN THE LITERATURE
Checked 2026-09-17, after the arity test.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN, AND IT IS A LOT

  Arora et al., Zoology (2312.04927) and Based (2402.18668) establish a RECALL-STATE-SIZE
  TRADEOFF on MQAR that holds within and across architecture families: increasing the
  recurrent state size almost always improves recall, attention gets perfect recall because
  its state grows with sequence length, and small-state models have limited recall capacity.
  This is the central result our work is adjacent to, and it is well established.

  Boesch & Wee (2609.16183), "Anatomy of Associative Recall in Fixed-State Recurrences",
  is the closest paper to what we did today, and it is ALREADY CITED IN THIS REPO
  (evidence/recall_distance.py, evidence/results/recall_curriculum.txt). It reports an
  INTERFERENCE WALL -- a sudden collapse to chance when retrieving 4 pairs among
  distractors -- and attributes it to SPARSE SUPERVISION rather than architectural capacity,
  with a distance curriculum taking accuracy from 0.021 to 1.000.

WHY THEIR WALL IS NOT OUR WALL

  They use MATRIX states (d_v x d_k, ~1024 elements) and their cells store 32 pairs at
  >= 0.99. So the factorisation we lack is already present in their architecture: their wall
  sits on top of a state that CAN select, and it is a training-coverage problem.

  Ours is structural and arrives at n = 3, with the value set still intact (in_set ~ 0.9).
  Their result corroborates the arity reading from the other side: give a recurrence
  separately addressable rows and 32 pairs are fine; give it one summed vector and 3 are too
  many.

THE GAP WE MAY BE SITTING IN, STATED CAUTIOUSLY

  A matrix state is BOTH bigger and factorised, so the literature's "state size" conflates
  size with factorisation. The fetched summary of 2609.16183 notes it "doesn't explicitly
  distinguish vector versus matrix organization as affecting filter availability".

  evidence/arity_test.py holds the state size FIXED at 64 numbers and varies only the
  factorisation: one factor of 64 against n slots of 64/n. The factorised arm wins 1.000 to
  0.195 at n = 8 while using NARROWER slots. If that survives scrutiny it separates two
  things the field currently reports as one -- and the direction is the opposite of the
  naive reading, since more room in one factor does not help while less room split into
  parts does.

  This is a claim to test further, not one to publish yet. In particular it is a
  by-construction result, and the trained-model version needs a matrix-state arm in this
  same codebase to confirm it.

ON DEPTH, WHICH SUPPORTS THE NESTED-ADDRESSING IDEA

  The transformer literature finds that the number of reasoning steps a model can perform
  IMPLICITLY is strictly limited by its depth, and that induction heads need two layers
  minimum. So "sequential selection is depth-bounded" is an established result rather than a
  guess, which is why the L = 2 / 4 / 6 runs are worth doing: 4 pairs needs 4.58 bits, and
  if each layer chains one selection then L = 6 is the first depth with enough budget.
